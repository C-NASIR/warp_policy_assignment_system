## Ask the question first

Use **assignment history** to answer: “What assignment was true for this employee at this time?” A history row carries its value, source, explanation snapshot, and effective timestamp interval. Its interval starts at `valid_from` and continues up to—but not including—`valid_to`.

Use the **audit log** to answer: “What changed, when, and who caused it?” Audit events record the actor, action, entity, UTC timestamp, and before/after details.

| Question | Best evidence |
| --- | --- |
| What was Avery's pay schedule before the override? | Assignment history |
| Which source supplied Monthly? | Assignment history and its explanation |
| Who created or removed the override? | Audit log |
| When did the change occur? | Audit log |
| Why did the policy-derived value return? | New assignment explanation plus the related audit event |

## Follow the override timeline

Before the override, Avery's open Pay schedule row is Bi-weekly. Committing Monthly closes that row and opens an override-sourced row. Removing the override closes Monthly and opens a newly resolved Bi-weekly row.

The audit log supplies the human actions around that timeline. It does not replace the assignment history, and history does not identify every administrative action.

A material explanation change can open a new history row even when the displayed value is unchanged. Compare source and evidence, not only the value label.
