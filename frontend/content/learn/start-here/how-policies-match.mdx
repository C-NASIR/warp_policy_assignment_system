## Direct matching

An active policy can become a candidate when its effective version's conditions match an employee's facts. Avery directly matches:

- California Pay Schedule because state is California.
- Standard PTO and Security Baseline because employee type is Full-time.
- California Compliance because state is California.
- Engineering Equipment because department is Engineering.

A direct explanation records the employee facts and condition clauses that matched.

## Group inheritance

Groups are explicit collections; PolicyOS does not infer Engineering group membership from Avery's Engineering department. A group manager adds Avery to the Engineering group and attaches Engineering Access to that group.

Engineering Access has a direct condition of `location = Remote`. Avery works in San Francisco, so that condition does not match. The policy still becomes a candidate because Avery is an explicit member of a group to which the policy is attached. The assignment explanation records the group origin instead of inventing a direct condition match.

This distinction matters when investigating a surprising value: check both the condition evidence and any group origin.

## One policy, one candidate set

If the same policy reaches an employee directly and through one or more groups, PolicyOS deduplicates it before resolution. Multiple paths do not multiply the policy's values.

Group membership changes use a preview. Attaching or detaching a policy from a group is a separate action and reconciles affected members immediately.
