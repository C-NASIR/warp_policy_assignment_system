## Two signals, one improvement loop

Each article asks whether it was helpful. A **Yes** or **Not yet** response and a fixed improvement category can be recorded in a connected workspace. Feedback contains no free-text response. Quick Find also records a search only when a signed-in reader pauses on a query with zero results. The submitting user's identity is not stored, and obvious email addresses and long numbers in missed queries are masked.

Demo mode stores neither signal. Learning activity is not part of the append-only product audit log: maintainers see aggregate learning signals beside that log because the same `audit:read` permission protects both views.

## Read the evidence carefully

The helpful percentage is directional, especially with few responses. “Articles needing attention” lists fixed-choice results by content ID. Unsuccessful searches combine the same normalized query and show its count and last occurrence.

The maintenance policy recommends acting after at least five article responses with more negative than positive votes, or after an in-scope query misses three times. A miss can call for a search alias or a clearer heading—not necessarily a new article.

## Ownership and due dates

Every article names a role accountable for behavior verification. Quarterly workflow reviews and semiannual stable-content reviews are enforced by the repository quality gate. An overdue review, missing implementation evidence, unregistered owner, or broken learning link fails the gate.

The article status stays in technical review until its feature owner and a representative reader complete the review workflow. Automated validation is evidence, but it does not impersonate organizational approval.
