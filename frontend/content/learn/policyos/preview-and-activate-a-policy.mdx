A policy definition only affects assignments when its status and effective dates allow it to participate.

Open **Standard Vacation** from Policies and inspect its status, effective date, **Who this applies to**, and **Assignments provided**.

If it is already **active** and effective today, continue to Rachel's assignments. There is no second activation step to perform.

If it is **draft** and you have activation authority:

1. Check that its condition and 15-day value express the intended rule.
2. Choose **Activate**.
3. Read the confirmation dialog. For an ordinary policy without automated roles, **Activate and reconcile** applies the change immediately.
4. Confirm when the definition is ready, then check the saved status and Rachel's current assignments.

Ordinary activation doesn't have a separate detailed impact-preview screen in this flow. The confirmation describes the action; it is not a list of every before-and-after assignment.

For a policy containing automated access roles, the lifecycle dialog instead offers **Preview and request approval**. That submits a request; the policy remains in its existing status until the approved change is executed. Continue through [Review controlled changes](/learn/policyos/review-controlled-changes).

The policy detail also distinguishes **Employees match the rule** from the number who **receive assignments from this policy**. These can differ when other rules or overrides determine the final values. The impact panel describes resolved behavior, not just the policy's intended output.

Check dates carefully. An active policy with only a future effective version need not change anyone today. The detail header shows the latest version, which may be future-dated; it isn't proof that this version is currently supplying assignments.

The related **Archive** and **Reactivate** actions change whether a policy participates. Archiving can cause employees to fall back to other policies or lose a value, so it is a substantive change, not a way to hide a row.

Missing lifecycle controls mean the record's current state or your permissions don't allow the action. An authorized colleague can complete activation without changing the rule's business meaning.
