A policy can stay the same while Rachel's circumstances change. Let's move her from Design to Product, which is available in the employee form.

Before editing, open Rachel's detail and note her department, current values, and their sources. In this example, Design Tools covers full-time employees in Design or Engineering, so Product will no longer meet that condition.

1. Choose **Edit employee**.
2. Change **Department** to **Product**, keeping her other facts unchanged.
3. Choose **Review assignments** to calculate the proposed result.
4. Compare the preview's resulting values with the current assignment cards you noted.
5. Choose **Confirm changes** when the new profile is intended.
6. Close the editor, revisit Rachel's profile and assignments, and check the saved department and resulting sources.

The connected employee preview displays the resulting values. Its cards are not a complete before-and-after removal list, and their “Add” labels shouldn't be read as proof that every displayed value is new. Compare the values with the existing record; use Assignment history after saving to inspect ended results.

Rachel should lose the Design tool only if no other policy, group, or override supplies it. Her vacation rule is based on tenure, so changing department alone doesn't remove that eligibility.

If you created Launch Tools with **Department = Product**, she now matches it directly. An existing Product Launch membership may provide another route to the same policy. This is a useful reason to inspect explanations even when a final value stays the same.

The editor requires employee-update permission. Preview and execution authority are separate, and employee and field scopes still apply. A manager change can also affect other people's derived reporting facts; reviewing only Rachel's visible cards may not show the entire population impact.

If execution reports that the preview is stale or expired, review the current record and calculate a new preview before confirming again. Don't assume the earlier result still describes the intended change.
