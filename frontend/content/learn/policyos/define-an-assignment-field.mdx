You want the company to assign an annual vacation allowance. First, give that decision a field of its own.

Open **Assignment fields**, either through Quick find's **Configure assignment fields** command or the link below. Search before creating a field: reuse an existing field only if it represents the same decision and has the required cardinality.

In a connected training deployment:

1. Choose **Create field**.
2. Enter **Annual vacation allowance** as the Field name.
3. Select **One value**. Rachel should have one final annual allowance.
4. Choose **Create field** to save it.
5. Find the new row and check that its Cardinality is **One value** and its Resolution is **Highest priority wins**.

This creates the output category. It doesn't assign vacation days to Rachel yet; a policy or override will supply the value.

Prepare **Application access** as a **Many values** field as well if an equivalent field doesn't already exist. Its Resolution displays **Set union**, meaning distinct values from applicable policies are combined.

For a one-value field, different values tied at the highest priority cause a conflict. The system doesn't choose an arbitrary answer just because the field accepts only one value.

**Cardinality is fixed after creation.** Decide whether the question expects one answer or several before saving. This screen doesn't provide a control to convert an existing field between them.

The form asks for a name and cardinality. For our vacation examples, we'll enter values such as `15 days` and `20 days` in the policy builder. Use consistent wording: these are stored assignment values, not an automatically calculated vacation balance.

Reading this catalog requires settings access; **Create field** requires settings-management permission. A role's assignment-field scope can also limit what it sees. If the field is missing, check access before creating a duplicate.

Your field is ready when it appears in the policy builder under **What gets assigned**.
