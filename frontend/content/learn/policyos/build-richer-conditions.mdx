Now express a rule with a narrower population:

> Give the design tool to full-time employees in Design or Engineering.

Use a separate training policy called **Design Tools** so Standard Vacation keeps its original purpose.

1. Open **Create policy**, name the policy, and choose its priority and effective date.
2. Set the outer **Employees matching** selector to **ALL**.
3. Make the outer condition **Employee type = Full-time**.
4. Choose **Add nested group** and set that group's selector to **ANY**.
5. Set its first condition to **Department = Design**.
6. Choose **Add nested condition** and add **Department = Engineering**.
7. Under **What gets assigned**, choose the many-value **Application access** field and enter **Design tool**.

Read the structure aloud: full-time **AND** (Design **OR** Engineering). Rachel matches because she is full-time and belongs to Design. A part-time designer doesn't match.

You can also use derived inputs. **Tenure >= 2 years** describes completed calendar service based on the employee's start date and the evaluation date. You don't enter a separate tenure value on Rachel's employee record. Manager-related inputs are also available through the condition catalog.

The current visual builder supports outer conditions and one nested group of conditions. It offers ALL/ANY and the comparisons allowed for each selected input; it has no general NOT control. More deeply nested existing rules aren't fully represented by this editor, so don't use it to recreate such a rule unless its entire structure is accounted for.

Choose **Preview impact** before saving, following the previous lesson's save and status checks. For a new policy, the browser's match estimate simplifies derived facts such as tenure and reporting relationships. Check the saved connected result, especially around anniversaries or indirect reporting relationships.

If you open **New version** on an existing policy instead, previewing follows the connected version-review workflow described later. Inspect every prefilled condition rather than assuming the editor copied an arbitrary rule tree completely.
