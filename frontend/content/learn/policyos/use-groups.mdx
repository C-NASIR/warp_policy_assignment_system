Rachel joins Product Launch. The group will provide a policy through explicit membership, even though her department is Design.

First prepare an active training policy called **Launch Tools**, using the earlier authoring workflow. Give it **Department = Product** as its direct condition and **Application access = Launch tool** as its output. This rule also directly covers Product employees; the group will add another route for Rachel.

Then open **Groups**:

1. Choose **Create group**, enter **Product Launch**, and choose **Create group** to save it.
2. On its detail page, find **Attached policies**, select **Launch Tools**, and choose **Attach**. This is an immediate change; it doesn't open a preview dialog.
3. Under **Members**, select Rachel and choose **Save Changes**.
4. Open Rachel's employee page and expand the Launch tool assignment. Look for **Product Launch** as the group origin.

Attaching the policy while the new group is empty separates setup from the membership change. When a group already has members, attaching or detaching a policy immediately reconciles those members.

The policy becomes a candidate for Rachel through the group even though her Design department doesn't match its direct Product condition. It still uses the same cardinality and priority rules as directly matched policies.

To remove Rachel, use the remove control beside her name and choose **Save Changes**. To remove a policy from everyone in the group, remove it from the draft and save the policy changes.

Removing a route doesn't necessarily remove a value. Rachel could still qualify through another group or a direct match. Verify her final assignment after the change.

Creating groups requires group-creation permission; editing membership and attachments requires group-update permission. The selectors also depend on your ability to read employees and policies. If a policy is absent, check that it is active, visible, and not already attached.
