Standard Vacation's definition says “15 days.” Now verify what Rachel actually receives.

1. Open **Employees**, search for Rachel, and select her name.
2. Find **Current assignments** and locate **Annual vacation allowance**.
3. Read the displayed value and source. With only the standard vacation rule active and no override, expect **15 days** from **Standard Vacation**.
4. Select the assignment card to expand its explanation.
5. Compare the displayed matched fact with Rachel's **Employee profile**. Here, the expected and actual employee type should be **Full-time**.

An assignment card includes the field, final value, source, and a **Policy** or **Override** badge. Expand it for a plain-language summary followed by match origins, candidate decisions, and decision details. Group routes list every contributing group. An override lists every policy value it replaced, or says that it created a previously unassigned value.

For Application access, several cards can be correct: a many-value field can hold both company email and a design tool. Don't interpret the number of assignment values as the number of policies or employees.

The candidate decision section shows the recorded winner and every relevant competitor with its value, priority, version, outcome, and origin. A lower-priority candidate is a losing value for a one-value field. A duplicate candidate for a many-value field means the value was kept once and the higher-priority policy was retained as its recorded source.

For a many-value field, the presence of a priority label doesn't mean only the highest-priority application survives. Distinct values combine; priority identifies the source when policies duplicate a value.

If Rachel's allowance differs from 15 days, check the source before changing anything. She may have an override, a higher-priority matching policy, or a group policy. If the value is absent, check policy status, effective dates, and the exact condition values.

Your account needs assignment-reading permission as well as access to Rachel. Field scope can hide particular assignment categories. An incomplete view is therefore not enough evidence that the organization has no assignment for that field.

You have completed the first full path through the application: an employee fact, a policy condition and output, and the resulting assignment.
