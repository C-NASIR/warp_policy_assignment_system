Rachel joins a product-launch team with people from several departments.

Their shared responsibility isn't captured by a single department condition. You need to name the people taking part.

You create a group called **Product Launch** and explicitly add its members, including Rachel.

**A group is a named collection of employees.** In PolicyOS, membership is managed explicitly. Giving a group the name “Design” does not automatically fill it with everyone whose department is Design.

You then attach a Launch Tools policy to Product Launch.

That connection gives the group's members another route to the policy:

> Rachel → Product Launch membership → attached Launch Tools policy.

We've now encountered two routes:

- **Direct match:** Rachel's facts satisfy a policy's conditions.
- **Group membership:** Rachel belongs to a group with that policy attached.

In PolicyOS, an attached policy becomes a candidate for every member even if they don't meet its direct conditions. This makes attaching a policy to a group a meaningful decision about who it covers.

The group itself supplies membership. The attached policy supplies the assignment values.

Those values still go through the same resolution rules. A group policy doesn't automatically outrank a directly matched policy. One-value fields still use priority, and many-value fields still combine distinct values.

If Rachel reaches the same policy directly and through two groups, the system considers that policy once, while retaining the reasons it reached her.

When the launch ends, removing her membership removes that route. She may still receive the same value through another group or a direct match, so the remaining sources must be considered again.

Next, we'll look at a decision intended for Rachel alone: an explicit exception.
