Rachel moves from Design to Operations. Her employee record now reflects the new department.

Before the move, her assignments included company email and the design tool. The design-tool policy requires membership in the Design department; the email policy covers all employees.

What should her assignments look like now?

**Reconciliation means calculating what the assignments should be, comparing that with what is recorded, and applying the differences.**

For this example, suppose no group or override supplies the design tool:

| Assignment | After the department change |
| --- | --- |
| Company email | Keep: the employee policy still covers Rachel |
| Design tool | End: the Design condition no longer matches |

If Rachel still belongs to Product Launch, that explicit membership continues until it is changed. Moving departments doesn't automatically remove her from every group.

The system must consider all the remaining routes before removing a value. Losing one source doesn't necessarily mean losing the assignment.

Reconciliation can be needed after several kinds of change:

- An employee fact changes.
- A policy, group membership, or override changes.
- A policy version reaches an effective-date boundary.
- An employee reaches a service anniversary used by a condition.

That last case matters: Rachel can complete two years of service without anyone editing her start date. Time itself changes the answer.

A system therefore needs both reactions to edits and processing for scheduled events. PolicyOS provides a worker for due events; a deployment must arrange for it to run regularly. A stored schedule alone doesn't update an assignment.

If the calculated result, source, and explanation are unchanged, reconciliation keeps the existing record. When they change, it preserves the previous record in history and records the new state.

Next, we'll consider how to inspect the effect of a change before applying it.
