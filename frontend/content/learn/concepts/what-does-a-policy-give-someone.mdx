Rachel works full time, so she matches this policy:

> Full-time employees get 15 vacation days each year.

We've understood the first half: who qualifies. Now look at the second half:

> 15 vacation days each year.

There are two pieces inside that answer: **what we're deciding** and **the value we choose**.

Here, we're deciding the annual vacation allowance. The chosen value is 15 days.

We call the thing being decided an **assignment field**. We call its answer an **assignment value**.

| Assignment field | Example value |
| --- | --- |
| Annual vacation allowance | 15 days |
| Pay schedule | Every two weeks |
| Application access | Company email |

Think of a field as a question with a name. “Pay schedule” asks how often someone gets paid. “Every two weeks” is one possible answer; “Monthly” is another.

The field tells us what kind of answer belongs there. A number of vacation days answers a different question from the name of an application.

This also separates the information we use from the decision we make:

- **Employee fact:** Rachel works full time.
- **Assignment field:** Annual vacation allowance.
- **Assignment value supplied by the policy:** 15 days.

The employment type helps us decide whether the rule applies. The vacation allowance is what the rule determines.

We can now read the whole policy precisely:

> If employment type is full-time, set annual vacation allowance to 15 days.

Next, we'll take that general rule and turn it into a result for Rachel. That result is an assignment.
