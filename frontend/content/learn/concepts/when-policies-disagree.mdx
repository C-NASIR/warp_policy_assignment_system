Rachel matches two policies that supply different annual vacation allowances. The field accepts one value, so we must decide which rule takes precedence.

Your company intends the experienced-employee allowance to take precedence over the standard allowance. You express that choice using **priority**.

| Policy | Candidate allowance | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard Vacation | 15 days | 10 |
| Experienced Employee Vacation | 20 days | 20 |

In PolicyOS, a higher priority number takes precedence. With these priorities, Rachel receives 20 days.

**Priority makes the company's intended preference explicit.** It doesn't make a policy match someone who fails its conditions.

Nor does the system automatically prefer a rule because its name sounds more specific. The company must express that preference in the priorities.

Now imagine someone changes Standard Vacation's priority to 20. Both policies are tied at the highest priority, but they disagree about the value.

That is an **unresolved conflict**. PolicyOS reports the conflict and refuses to resolve the proposed result until the disagreement is addressed.

You can correct the priorities to reflect the intended preference, change the conditions so the rules don't overlap, or correct a value if one was entered incorrectly. The right fix depends on what the company actually intends.

If tied policies both supply 20 days, they agree on the result. That is a duplicate value, not a disagreement about Rachel's allowance. PolicyOS uses a stable tie-break to record its source.

**Deterministic behavior** means the same inputs and rules lead to the same outcome. That outcome can be a clear conflict requiring a decision; it doesn't have to be an arbitrary winner.
