Before putting a vacation increase into effect, you want to understand whom it will affect.

Rachel may be one of many employees covered by the policy. Some may have overrides. Others may match a higher-priority rule.

Simply counting matching employees won't tell you whose final allowance changes.

A **preview** calculates the effect of a proposed change without committing it. It helps answer questions such as:

- Which assignments would be added, ended, or replaced?
- Whose final values would stay the same?
- Would the change create a conflict?

Suppose the preview shows Rachel moving from 20 days to 22. Another employee's 25-day override remains in place. Both employees can match the changed policy while their final results behave differently.

Preview and saving answer different questions:

| Step      | Question                                                     |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Preview   | What would happen if this exact proposal were committed now? |
| Save      | Has the proposed change actually been committed?             |

A preview is evidence, not a saved change. If the underlying record or calculated impact changes before saving, the earlier preview can become stale. Calculate again instead of assuming the old result still applies.

**First understand the effect. Then commit the change that was actually reviewed.**
