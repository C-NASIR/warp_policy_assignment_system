Before putting a vacation increase into effect, you want to understand whom it will affect.

Rachel may be one of many employees covered by the policy. Some may have overrides. Others may match a higher-priority rule.

Simply counting matching employees won't tell you whose final allowance changes.

A **preview** calculates the effect of a proposed change without committing it. It helps answer questions such as:

- Which assignments would be added, ended, or replaced?
- Whose final values would stay the same?
- Would the change create a conflict?

For a change effective today, suppose the preview shows Rachel moving from 20 days to 22. Another employee's 25-day override remains in place. Both employees can match the changed policy while their final results behave differently.

Some changes also require **approval**: an authorized person reviews the proposal and decides whether it may proceed.

**Execution** is the step that actually commits the approved change. Approval alone doesn't change Rachel's assignment. If execution creates a future version, its future date still governs when its values apply.

These steps answer different questions:

| Step | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Preview | What would happen? |
| Approval | May this proposed change proceed? |
| Execution | Has the change actually been committed? |

In PolicyOS, ordinary signed-in authors use independent approval for new policy versions and automated-access lifecycle changes. Employee edits and override reviews have their own preview-and-confirm flows. Where independent approval applies, the author cannot approve their own request, and the approving user must execute it with the required authority.

A preview also depends on the state that was reviewed. If relevant data changes, or an approval expires, the system may reject execution and require a fresh preview and approval.

**Review the proposed effect, then make sure the committed change is the one that was reviewed.**
