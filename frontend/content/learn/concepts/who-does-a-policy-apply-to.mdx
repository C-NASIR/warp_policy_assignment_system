Your company has a new rule:

> Full-time employees get 15 vacation days each year.

Does it apply to Rachel?

You can't answer that from her name. You need to know whether she works full time.

You look at her employee record and find:

> Employment type: Full-time.

That is an **employee fact**: a piece of information describing Rachel. Her department and start date are other examples.

Now compare the fact with the rule:

- The policy requires the employee to be full-time.
- Rachel is full-time.
- Rachel meets the requirement, so the policy applies to her.

That requirement is the policy's **condition**.

**A fact describes the employee. A condition tells us what must be true for a policy to apply.**

Here, the fact is “Rachel's employment type is full-time.” The condition is “employment type must be full-time.” Comparing them gives us a match.

Try the same comparison for a part-time employee. Their employment type doesn't meet this condition, so this policy doesn't match them. This rule alone says nothing about their vacation allowance; you would need to look at the rules that cover them.

The same approach works for other questions. A policy for the Design department needs a department fact. A policy for employees hired before a certain date needs a start date.

**To decide whether a policy applies, compare what it requires with what you know about the employee.**

We now know that Rachel matches our vacation policy. Next, we'll look more closely at what that policy gives her.
