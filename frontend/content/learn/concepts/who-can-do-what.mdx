Rachel has an employee record because the company needs to make decisions about her employment.

Does that mean she can sign in and edit company policies?

To answer that, we need to separate an **employee record** from a **user account**.

The employee record holds facts used in policy decisions. A user account represents someone who can sign in. They can be linked, but creating an employee record alone doesn't create a login or give someone administrative authority.

Once Rachel has a user account, two questions follow:

1. Who is signing in?
2. What is that person allowed to do?

**Authentication** establishes identity. **Authorization** controls permitted actions and access to data.

A **permission** allows a particular action, such as reading assignments, editing employee facts, or approving a change.

A **role** bundles permissions for a responsibility. An employee role might allow Rachel to read her own assignments. A people-operations role might allow a colleague to manage employee records.

The action is only part of the decision. **Scope** describes which records or assignment fields that access covers.

“Can read assignments” might mean Rachel's own assignments, her team's assignments, or everyone's. Access may also be limited to particular fields, such as application access. A role needs the appropriate permissions and data scope together.

In PolicyOS, users can have several roles. Their allowed permissions and scopes combine, so adding a broad role can expand access. A narrower role doesn't cancel a broader grant.

Some roles can be granted through policies to linked user accounts. These are distinct from roles assigned explicitly by an administrator. Losing a policy-derived grant doesn't remove an independently assigned role.

An assignment saying “company email” also doesn't itself create an email account. Actual access requires the corresponding provisioning or authorization mechanism.

You've now followed the domain from one decision for Rachel to reusable rules, resolved assignments, controlled changes, and explainable history.
