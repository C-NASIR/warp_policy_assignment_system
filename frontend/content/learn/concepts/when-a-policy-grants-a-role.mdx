Explicit role assignment works well when an administrator knows exactly who should receive a responsibility. Sometimes the same eligibility rule already exists in a policy.

Suppose the company has an **Engineering assignment reader** role. An access administrator marks that configured role as eligible for policy automation. A policy author can then include it as an automated role granted by an Engineering policy.

For Rachel to receive that role, the chain must be complete:

> Rachel's employee facts or group route make the policy apply → the policy grants an eligible role → Rachel's active user account is linked to her employee record

The policy does not create Rachel's account. Without a linked user account, Rachel can receive normal employee assignments from the policy but there is no PolicyOS user to receive the role.

Policy-derived and explicit grants remain separate:

- **Explicit grant:** an administrator assigns the role directly to Rachel's user account.
- **Policy-derived grant:** reconciliation grants the role because the linked employee currently reaches an effective policy version that supplies it.

If Rachel stops qualifying, reconciliation removes the policy-derived grant. If an administrator also assigned the same role explicitly, that explicit grant remains. Effective permissions and scopes are the union of both sources.

Only roles marked policy eligible appear for policy authoring. PolicyOS rejects automation for roles containing protected Root, access-management, access-review, or credential-management authority. Ordinary users' versions and lifecycle changes still follow the independent approval workflow, where reviewers can inspect projected role grants and removals.

An assignment value called “Company email” and a PolicyOS access role are also different. The first is an employee assignment result. The second authorizes actions inside PolicyOS. Neither provisions an account or permission in an external service.

Use policy-derived roles when access should follow policy eligibility inside PolicyOS. Use explicit roles when an administrator intends the grant to remain independent of that eligibility.

You can now explain how a workspace grows from one Root account into an organization of employees, users, roles, scopes, managers, authors, and reviewers. The PolicyOS section shows how these ideas appear in the application.
