## 1. Check the policy lifecycle

Only an active policy participates in resolution. Draft and archived policies contribute no assignments.

## 2. Check the effective version

Confirm that one policy version covers the date you are investigating. Ranges are inclusive, and a valid gap means the policy contributes no behavior during that gap.

## 3. Check how the policy could apply

For a direct match, compare every condition with the employee's current trusted or derived facts. For group inheritance, confirm both the employee's explicit membership and the policy attachment. A group-attached policy can apply even when its direct condition tree does not match.

## 4. Check whether another result won

“Matched” and “selected” are different. A higher-priority candidate can win a one-value field, while a manual override replaces the field's policy results. Equal-priority policies producing different values create a conflict instead of an arbitrary winner.

## 5. Check access boundaries

Employee scope and assignment-field scope can hide records and outputs. Direct requests for out-of-scope resources may return not found to avoid disclosing that the record exists.
